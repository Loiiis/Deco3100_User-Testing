Who can buy the house first in Sydney?

How social class shapes home ownership opportunities

Rising house prices affect everyone, but not everyone starts from the same place.

Introduction

Sydney is one of Australia's most economically prosperous cities, yet significant social class inequalities persist (Australian Bureau of Statistics [ABS], 2021). Social class influences an individual's life chances, including access to education, employment, income, and housing (Weber, 1978; Bourdieu, 1986).

In the housing market, property prices in Sydney have increased substantially over the past few decades, making home ownership increasingly difficult for younger generations (Property Carto, 2026).

However, not everyone faces the same challenges in entering the housing market. Individuals from different social classes possess unequal access to financial resources, educational opportunities, and family support, all of which can influence their ability to purchase a home.

By examining socioeconomic indicators and housing data across different areas of Sydney, this website explores who can buy a house first in Sydney and how social class continues to shape home ownership opportunities in contemporary Australia.

Part 1: The Housing Crisis in Sydney

- Why is buying a house becoming harder?

Housing prices in Sydney have risen substantially over the past several decades, significantly outpacing growth in household incomes and contributing to declining housing affordability among younger generations.

Research also shows that parental financial support has become increasingly important for young Australians entering the housing market (Parkinson et al., 2019). Young Australians today are often more dependent on parental assistance to purchase a home than previous generations.

However, not everyone receives the same financial assistance from their parents.

Families from more affluent socioeconomic backgrounds are typically able to provide more financial support, while disadvantaged families usually have fewer resources.

This raises an important question: Do all young people face the same challenges in buying a home?

Part 2: Different Starting Points

Do all young people face the same challenges in buying a home?

IRSAD (Index of Relative Socio-economic Advantage and Disadvantage) is a composite index developed by the Australian Bureau of Statistics to measure the relative socioeconomic conditions of an area. It combines indicators such as: income, education, employment, and occupation to identify areas with higher levels of advantage or disadvantage (ABS, 2021).

Does a higher IRSAD score correspond to higher educational attainment?

Residents in socioeconomically advantaged areas are more than twice as likely to hold a bachelor degree or higher qualification than residents in disadvantaged areas. Higher educational attainment is associated with better employment opportunities and higher incomes, increasing the capacity to enter the housing market (ABS, 2021).

Educational attainment and income explain part of the inequality in homeownership. However, research suggests that family background continues to matter even after individuals achieve similar educational qualifications and earnings (Mendolia & Siminski, 2016). This means family background can provide a significant advantage when purchasing a home.

Conclusion

In Sydney, young people from socioeconomically advantaged families are generally able to purchase homes earlier than those from disadvantaged backgrounds. While rising house prices affect all Australians, access to education, income, and intergenerational wealth provides some groups with a significant head start. In contemporary Sydney, home ownership is shaped not only by individual effort, but also by the social class into which a person is born.
